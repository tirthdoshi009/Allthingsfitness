# Atomic Habits

- **Author:** James Clear
- **Category:** Habits / Mindset
- **Status:** Finished
- **Rating:** ⭐️⭐️⭐️⭐️⭐️

## Why I picked it up

Fitness has never failed me on knowledge — it has failed me on consistency. This book is about
consistency rather than about training, which is exactly the gap I keep falling into.

## Big ideas

1. **Systems beat goals.** A goal is the outcome you want; the system is what you repeat every
   day. Two people can share the same goal and get opposite results because their daily systems
   differ. "Get fit" is not a plan; "gym at 7am on Mon/Wed/Fri" is.
2. **Make the first rep tiny.** The hardest part of a workout is starting it, so shrink the entry
   cost until refusing feels silly. Ten minutes on the mat beats an hour that never happens.
3. **Design the environment.** Willpower is unreliable, friction is not. Shoes by the door and a
   packed gym bag do more for attendance than motivation does.
4. **Identity drives behaviour.** Aim to become "a person who trains" rather than to complete a
   twelve-week challenge. Every session is a small vote for that identity.
5. **Never miss twice.** One skipped day is noise; two in a row is the start of a new pattern.

## What I'm actually going to do

- [ ] Fix three non-negotiable training slots in the calendar and treat them like meetings.
- [ ] Keep the minimum viable session to 15 minutes so a bad day still counts.
- [ ] Pack the gym bag the night before, next to the front door.
- [ ] Log every session in this repo so the streak is visible.

## Where it didn't convince me

The examples lean heavily on habits that are easy to start and cheap to repeat. Hard conditioning
work is genuinely unpleasant, and "make it attractive" only stretches so far — some days it is
simply discipline.

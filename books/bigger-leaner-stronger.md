# Bigger Leaner Stronger

- **Author:** Michael Matthews
- **Published:** 2012, second edition 2019
- **Category:** Strength / Nutrition
- **Status:** Reading
- **Rating:** 3 out of 5

**One line:** One opinionated beginner plan, arguing that progressive overload on compound
lifts plus a protein target is most of what a first few years of training requires.

## Why I picked it up

I wanted one opinionated, beginner friendly plan instead of ten conflicting opinions from
the internet.

## The core argument

Matthews' position is that the beginner's problem is not a lack of information but too much
of it, and that the correct response is a narrow plan followed for long enough to produce
data. The training half reduces to progressive overload on a handful of compound lifts. The
nutrition half reduces to energy balance deciding weight and protein intake deciding how
much of the change is muscle. Everything else, exercise variety, meal timing, supplements,
is treated as a rounding error until the basics have been run consistently for months.

The structural claim is that consistency dominates optimization at this level. A simple
plan followed for a year beats a sophisticated one abandoned in six weeks, so the plan
should be designed for adherence rather than for theoretical completeness.

## Ideas worth keeping

- **Progressive overload is the whole game.** If the numbers in the log are not moving, the
  program is not working, no matter how hard the session felt.
- **Compound lifts first.** Squat, deadlift, bench and overhead press give the most return
  per minute spent. Isolation work is a garnish, not the meal.
- **Calories decide weight, protein protects muscle.** Energy balance sets the direction,
  protein and lifting decide the composition of the change.
- **Boring beats clever** when you are far from your ceiling.
- **Track something.** Weights, reps and body weight are cheap to record and make progress
  arguable rather than a feeling.

## Where I disagree, or where it is thin

The supplement discussion is more confident than the evidence warrants. The tone also
assumes a reader whose main constraint is effort, where mine is usually time, and the book
has little to say about fitting training into a week that fights back. Recovery and sleep
get much less room than they earn.

## What I will actually do

- [ ] Run a three day upper, lower, full body split for the next eight weeks.
- [ ] Log every set and add weight only when all target reps are clean.
- [ ] Hit a consistent daily protein target rather than micromanaging every meal.

## Verdict

Good for a first year, and honest about being that. If you have already read Rippetoe or
run a linear progression, the summary above is enough.
